Lake Te Koo Utu

About this walk

Lake Te Koo Utu (also spelt Te Ko Utu or Te Koutu) is a small lake right in the middle of Cambridge. It sits in a deep bowl surrounded by big old trees and native bush, so it feels quiet even though it's only a few minutes from the main street.

The lake formed nearly 2,000 years ago, when material from a Taupō eruption came down the Waikato River and blocked off the stream here. The lake was an important place for local Māori to catch eels and kōura (freshwater crayfish). The path around the lake is wide and flat with no steps.

Video description: A 16 minute walk around Lake Te Koo Utu. It follows the gravel path under tall trees and past lawns, weeping willows and water lilies, crosses a wooden bridge, passes a mother duck with her ducklings on the grass, and goes between tree ferns and big trees around the lake.

Things to look out for

  • The water lilies covering parts of the lake
  • Ducks and ducklings on the grass by the water
  • The huge old trees around the edge of the lake
  • The wooden bridge over one arm of the lake
  • Tree ferns growing right beside the water

Location: Cambridge Domain, Cambridge (entry off Albert Street)

Time: About 30 minutes around the lake (1.5 km)

Tips: The path is flat with no steps. There is a playground and toilets near the car park.
